PROGRESS

Moving forward, getting stuck and overcoming difficulties

WARM-UP

Are you making progress?

We all experience success, difficulties and moments when nothing seems to work. Today, we will learn idioms that help us talk about progress.

Moving forward

Making progress, reaching goals and finding solutions.

Getting stuck

Facing problems, wasting time or making little progress.

Discuss these questions

  1. What helps you make progress when you are learning something new?
  2. What do you usually do when you feel stuck?
  3. Can you think of a time when you were successful after a difficult period?

TASK 3 · SPEAKING

The Progress Meeting

Imagine you are part of a team working on an important project. Your manager has called a meeting to discuss progress. Choose a situation, explain the problem and suggest solutions.

Situation 1 — No progress

Your team has spent weeks discussing the same issue. Nobody agrees on a solution, and the deadline is approaching.

go around in circles get to grips with make headway get your act together

Your task: Explain why the team is not making progress and suggest two practical solutions.

Questions from the manager
  • Why are we going around in circles?
  • What do we need to get to grips with first?
  • How can we start making headway?

Situation 2 — Things are improving

Your team has completed several important tasks and received positive feedback. More customers are becoming interested in your product.

be on a roll gain ground be on the right track light at the end of the tunnel

Your task: Describe the team's success and explain what you should do next.

Questions from the manager
  • What makes you think we're on the right track?
  • How has our product been gaining ground?
  • How can we keep this success going?

Situation 3 — A difficult decision

The budget has been cut, deadlines are approaching, and the current strategy isn't working. The team must decide whether to continue, change direction or pause the project.

be flogging a dead horse be barking up the wrong tree put something on hold be in the pipeline

Your task: Recommend a course of action and justify your decision.

Questions from the manager
  • Are we barking up the wrong tree?
  • Should we put the project on hold?
  • What alternative plans do we have in the pipeline?
